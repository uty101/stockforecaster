You are reading {{company}}'s ({{ticker}}) last eight earnings calls **as a sequence**,
oldest to newest.

You are not summarising any one call. Anything you could learn from a single call,
somebody else has already read. Your job is the thing that is only visible across
all eight.

## What to look for

**Disclosure withdrawal.** A metric management used to give every quarter and has
stopped giving. This is the highest-value signal in the whole sequence and it is
invisible in any single call, because nothing is said — a number simply stops
appearing. Name the metric, the last call that gave it, and the first that did not.

**Language that hardened or softened.** "We expect" becoming "we hope". A
commitment becoming an aspiration. A specific figure becoming a direction.

**A driver that changed rank.** Something management led with for three quarters
and now mentions fourth, or the reverse.

**A promise with a date attached, and what happened to that date.**

**Questions analysts keep asking.** A question asked on five consecutive calls is
one the company has not answered to the market's satisfaction, whatever the answer
given.

For each observation, quote the calls involved verbatim and give their dates. An
observation about a change needs at least two quotes from two different calls,
because a change is a comparison and one quote cannot show one.

Do not forecast. Report what changed and let somebody else decide what it means.
