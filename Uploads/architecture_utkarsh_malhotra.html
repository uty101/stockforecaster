Agents vs Wall Street

Alpha

Nine lenses read the same evidence and cannot see each other. Every claim carries a quote that is matched back to its filing. One judge weighs what survives by how much each driver is worth, not by how many lenses agreed.

How it works

One command runs four companies end to end. Each company walks the same stages in the same order.

That order is written top to bottom in forecaster/run.py. There is no registry and no dispatch loop, so the whole data flow can be read aloud while somebody watches over your shoulder.

Every box below has a stable identifier. J1 to J7 for sources, U1 to U26 for the pipeline. They are defined once in forecaster/nodes.py and they appear in the module names, the event names and the log lines. A line in the log names the box that produced it.

The workstreams column above is the nine lenses. Lens is the name used in the code, so both words appear in this page and they mean the same thing.

What the agents do

There are seventeen agents, sitting in seven layers.

One pulls candidate figures out of the filings. One pulls the guidance range out, because guidance is written in sentences. One is a research scout, which suggests searches, looks at what came back, then asks for whatever it is still missing. And two scanners, one reading the calls in sequence, the other reading coverage.

Then nine lenses do the actual analysis. Each one has a single angle.

  • Guidance anchors on what management guided and where this company usually lands in its own range.
  • Drivers builds the top line bottom up, units times price.
  • Margins takes revenue as given and argues about what falls through.
  • Forensics reads like an auditor, accruals versus cash.
  • Peer read looks at who has already reported.
  • Demand goes one link up and one link down the value chain.
  • Market splits growth into market growth and share.
  • Macro checks the series against what the Street seems to be assuming.
  • Mechanical is just arithmetic. FX, share count, net interest.

How they are connected

They are not. That is the point.

All nine read the same evidence and none of them can see what the others said. That is in the code, in the signatures, not in the prompt. There is no way to pass one lens's answer to another.

Because if they can see each other they converge, and once they converge you have just rebuilt consensus.

The only thing sitting underneath all nine is a gate.

Tiers and the client

Tiers are assigned once, in forecaster/agents/tiers.py. Cheap for retrieval and extraction, mid for the lenses, the challenge and the narrative, deep for the judge alone. One deep call per company, enforced by a test.

The client is our own urllib wrapper over the Messages API, since there is no working pip on the build machine. It does five things. It forces a schema and validates before returning. It caches on a content hash on disk. It enforces a hard cost ceiling. It handles prompt caching of the shared corpus. It retries on rate limits and 5xx only.

Research and financial reasoning

Where the information comes from

Five source adapters sit behind one priority chain.

The organisers' frozen corpus of 1,139 documents supplies filings, transcripts and slides. It is the only source of historical financials. A number that traces to a document a judge can open is worth more than one tracing to an API response they cannot see.

On top of that sits a research agent. It proposes searches, looks at the titles that came back, then asks for what is still missing. A fixed list of queries cannot notice that every result came from the same trade magazine.

News is date bounded at the API rather than filtered afterwards, so anything published after the lock date never enters the result set. Macro comes from FRED with realtime_start pinned to the lock date, so a series arrives as it stood then rather than as later revised. Consensus is shown beside every forecast and never feeds one.

Everything pulled from the web is written into the run directory as a document. A lens citing research is quoting text that exists on disk, and the gate can match it exactly as it matches a quote from a results release.

Metrics and units

The metric labels, units and period headers come from the templates and challenge/companies.json. The agent never invents them.

Every extracted value is typed against the metric it is offered for. Three guards reject values that merely mention the right words. A percentage offered for a money or per share metric is discarded. A post exceptional value offered for a pre exceptional line is discarded. A figure covering the wrong period length is discarded, so a half year figure cannot satisfy a full year metric.

Each rejection is recorded on the metric rather than dropped quietly.

Consensus revenue arrives in absolute currency and is converted to millions in exactly one place. Getting that direction wrong is an error of a million times that still looks like a plausible number on a sheet.

Conflicting evidence

Four mechanisms, in order.

First, the evidence store splits verification three ways. Prose means a person wrote it and a model quoted it, so the quote has to be found in the text. Structured means the quote is the tagged fact itself, rendered by an adapter from a typed response. Derived means the pipeline computed it, so it sits in neither set and has to be justified by the claims underneath it.

News is not evidence at all. We hold a snippet rather than the article, coverage published days before a print is preview and positioning, and no lens may cite it.

Second, every surviving case is argued into its strongest honest form and then attacked. The gap between the stated confidence and the surviving confidence reaches the judge as a discount on that view's weight. Never as a vote.

Third, the spread across lenses is measured per metric, with abstentions counted separately rather than folded in.

Fourth, the comparability gate asks whether this period can be compared with the company's own history at all. When it fires, the historical priors have stopped applying, and that is carried forward rather than noted and forgotten.

How it feeds the model

Before any of that runs, there is a deterministic income model built off the filings. History as filed, a ratio base, a projection.

It also re forecasts the last few periods using only what it knew before each one, so it tells you how wrong it has been. That error goes to the lenses as context and to the judge alongside the projection.

So a lens is looking at a company with a margin trend in front of it, not just a pile of numbers. And the judge knows how much to trust the arithmetic before it decides anything.

It is an income statement rather than three statements, because none of the twelve metrics touches a balance sheet or cash flow line.

How that drives the estimates

Each lens comes back with all three metrics in one call. Its reasoning, its confidence, its citations, and what would change its mind.

It is allowed to say nothing. Abstaining is a real answer and it beats making something up.

Then the gate. Every citation has to already exist, and every quote has to actually match the document. If a lens fails either one, the whole lens is dropped. Not the bad claim, the whole thing. Hays lost two of nine that way.

How they argue

They do not argue with each other. They get argued against.

Every surviving view goes to a champion stage that builds the case up as strongly as it honestly can, then turns round and attacks it properly. What comes out the other side is a surviving confidence, and it is usually a lot lower than what the lens claimed for itself.

That happens before anything gets compared. Because if you just line up the raw findings and take the majority, you are rewarding whatever was easiest to spot.

How it becomes the figures, and the weighting

Then one Opus call, once per company. It gets every surviving view, the confidence after it has been attacked, which lenses went quiet and why, and how much they disagree.

Weighting is materiality. For each metric I measure what one standard deviation of each driver is actually worth, by nudging it and re running the model. That is the weight. It is measured off the company's own history rather than decided by me.

Surviving confidence then discounts it. It is never a vote.

So six lenses agreeing on something weak still lose to one lens holding the company's own guidance with a quote. They are all reading the same documents anyway, so five of them agreeing is about as likely to be wrong as one.

The judge returns five quantiles per metric. The median is what goes in the workbook. Everything else is on the page.

Important design choices

Positioning toward consensus is switched off by configuration, not deleted

Accuracy here is our miss divided by the Street's miss, capped at 5.0 and averaged over twelve metrics. A forecast shrunk onto consensus inherits the Street's error and scores about 1.0 by construction. Safe, and unable to place.

Submitting our own estimate is the only way to score below 1.0. It accepts a wider spread of outcomes in exchange.

This reverses the specification's own instinct, so it is written down rather than left to be inferred from a weight that happens to equal 1. Consensus is still acquired, still carried on its own path, and every regime condition it would have triggered is still measured. The run states, for each metric, the weight that would have applied.

The rejected alternative was deleting the positioning stage, which would have made the decision invisible.

Consensus travels on its own path

It is tapped once where consensus enters, carried untouched, and handed to exactly one stage. It exposes no method a reasoning stage could write to, and no lens or judge signature takes it. A test asserts both properties.

So lookahead through consensus is prevented by the shape of the code rather than by a promise in a prompt.

The same instinct governs the lenses. There is no parameter through which one view could reach another, and a change that would create one is rejected on that basis alone.

One fabricated citation drops the whole lens

Not the offending claim, the entire view.

A lens that invented one citation has told you something about the rest of its reasoning. Keeping its surviving claims means keeping the parts of an argument whose author you have just stopped trusting.

The cost is real. Hays lost two of nine this way on the final run. The alternative, dropping the bad claim and keeping the rest, was rejected because it makes fabrication cheap.

One deep call, spent on the judge

The judge is the highest leverage decision in the system, so it is the only call on the deep tier and exactly one is allowed per run.

The narrative agent is deliberately mid tier. Prose written from a settled answer is not where the leverage is, and spending the expensive tier there would take the call away from the decision that actually moves the number.

Degrade loudly, never quietly

If the judge call fails, the fallback is the median of surviving views and it labels itself degraded on every metric.

If the judge returns crossed quantiles, they are sorted and the correction is recorded. Accepting them silently and sorting them silently are both ways of hiding that it happened.

If a metric has no judged distribution, it falls back to the model projection and says so.

A blank cell scores the maximum 5.0 penalty, so the system always ships a number. It just never ships one that looks better than it is.

The newest healthy run wins, not the newest run

This exists because of a specific failure during the build.

The API ran out of credit mid run. Deere's research, all three scanners and eight of its nine lenses failed. The pipeline correctly degraded and produced numbers anyway. Those numbers then overwrote a healthy run's numbers, because the writer took the most recent run rather than the best one.

Health is now judged explicitly on the stage reached, a surviving lens floor of four, no failed lenses, a judge that did not degrade, and no metric equal to consensus. When nothing is healthy the writer says so loudly rather than quietly shipping the wreckage.

Standard library only, and no install step

No pip works on the build machine, so the client, schema validator, tier router, disk cache and fan out runner are written here over urllib.

That is a constraint rather than a preference, but it has one real benefit at 17:30. The final run has no dependency install that can fail.

Node is used only for writing the workbooks and checking their shape, where SheetJS was already in the starter.

Two things built and then removed

The SEC XBRL adapters work and are out of the priority chain on purpose, so every historical number traces to a document in this repository.

An earlier build computed a baseline of consensus times the company's shrunk historical beat. It was removed. Consensus is the bar this event actually scores against, and a second invented bar beside it was one more number a reader had to interpret first.

Checks and tests

What the running system catches

  • Fabricated quotes, twice. At extraction, every quote is matched back against the document it was attributed to. This is the only stage that catches a guidance range assembled from two different sentences, which reads beautifully and is not what the company said. Then again at the gate, deterministically, because nothing that can hallucinate should decide which hallucinations survive.
  • Invented citation identifiers. Every cited identifier must already exist in the evidence store. An estimate with no citation at all is also dropped, since an uncited estimate cannot be checked.
  • Total collapse. If every lens is dropped, the run stops rather than emitting a number nothing supports.
  • Bad units and mixed kinds. Percentage for money, post exceptional for pre exceptional, and wrong period length values are rejected before they can reach a median.
  • Implausible distributions. Crossed quantiles are sorted and flagged. Disagreement is measured per metric. The comparability gate flags periods where the priors have broken.
  • Invented figures in the write up. Every number in the narrative agent's prose is pulled out and checked against the figures the pipeline actually produced. Prose carrying an invented figure is rejected rather than printed.
  • Malformed model responses. The schema validator never coerces, never defaults a missing field, and never accepts a string where a float was declared. Schema errors fail fast, since retrying malformed output mostly reproduces it and bills you twice. Rate limits and 5xx are retried.
  • Cost. A hard ceiling stops the run before the call that would breach it.
  • Malformed workbooks. The writer patches the supplied templates in place. It refuses a non finite value, a value count that does not match the metric count, a template that no longer matches companies.json, or one whose period header does not match the specification. The checker then reopens each file on disk and verifies the labels, the units, the period header, and that every forecast cell holds a finite number.
  • Whole company failure. Each company is retried once. The retry is nearly free because every repeated model call is served from the disk cache, so what is really being retried is the transport. One company failing cannot cost the other three their numbers.

What was tested, and what happened

106 automated tests, all passing. 93 Python and 13 Node, verified at the time of writing.

Beyond ordinary unit coverage, a set of structural tests asserts the architecture rather than the outputs. No lens signature can accept another lens's view. The corpus a lens reads contains no lens output. Only the positioning stage accepts consensus. Consensus exposes nothing writable. Only the judge runs on the deep tier. Every lens dropped stops the run rather than forecasting. The results file is never observed half written. Every prompt file has a node, every agent node has a prompt, and every prompt declares a version.

Three defects were found by these checks during the build, each fixed in its own commit.

  • Hays net fees were contaminated. A results release gives net fees in pounds and net fee growth as a percentage in the same breath. Both mention net fees. Both matched on the label. Fourteen of sixteen observations in the series were percentage declines sitting in a series measured in millions. A median across two kinds of thing is a median of nothing, and everything downstream inherited it. This is what the kind and period length guards now exist for.
  • The judge's metrics were silently unmatched. The judge is given each metric label with its units beside it, and sometimes echoes the pair back as one string. An exact comparison found nothing, so every metric read as unjudged. Label matching is now normalised, with a containment fallback.
  • A degraded run overwrote a healthy one after the API ran out of credit. This is the failure that produced the run health check above.

The final run reports an exit code that means something. 0 four workbooks written, verified, and every number forecast in this run. 1 a workbook failed verification, do not upload. 2 the workbooks are the right shape but at least one company carried forward previous numbers rather than forecasting today. The last clean run exited 0 with all four files passing.

Reproducing the run

Install and configure. Python 3.14, standard library only, so there is nothing to install. Node.js with npm install for the two packages the starter already listed.

Three keys in .env or the environment. ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is required. EXA_API_KEY covers news and industry research. FRED_API_KEY covers macro. Missing optional keys degrade that source and are recorded on the run rather than crashing it.

How the companies and metrics enter. Nothing about the four companies is hardcoded in Python.

challenge/companies.json supplies the tickers, the fiscal periods, the twelve metric labels, their units and the output filenames. The supplied templates provide the period header. config/forecaster.json holds the run parameters: the as of date, the corpus freeze date, the source priority chain, the per company research angles and macro series, the tier to model mapping, the cost ceiling and the positioning switch.

Adding a fifth company means editing JSON, not Python.

Remaining human input. None during the run. A person reads the log, sanity checks the four numbers, and uploads the files by hand, since the rules require manual upload and the agent does not post to OpenStocks.

Known weaknesses

  • Dropping a lens whole is expensive. Two Hays lenses died on two failed quote matches, taking their good claims with them. The trade is deliberate, but a lens that mis transcribes one character is treated exactly like one that fabricates, and I would separate those cases next.
  • The extraction rejections are not audited. Between 43 and 144 candidate values per company were discarded by the guards. Spot checks say they are overwhelmingly correct, but nothing verifies that a right value was never thrown away with them.
  • The positioning base is asserted, not fitted. The regression that would measure it needs the Street bar as it stood at past quarters, and no source available here publishes consensus history. Positioning is switched off for this run, so it does not touch the submitted numbers, but the machinery would need that measurement before it should ever be switched on.
  • Hays has no consensus coverage at all in the market adapter, so three of the twelve numbers have no Street comparison to show. Hays is also the name where the corpus is thinnest, and it reports half yearly rather than quarterly.
  • Only the model's error is measured, not the system's. The reproduction check measures the deterministic projection against reported history. There is no backtest of the full nine lens pipeline, because doing it honestly needs point in time consensus and a corpus rebuilt per past quarter, and neither exists inside a one day event.
  • The judge is a single point of judgement. One deep call per company. If it fails, the fallback is a median of surviving views, which is labelled degraded but is a materially weaker answer.
  • The corpus is frozen at 2026-08-14 and the run is 2026-08-16. The two day gap is covered only by date bounded web search, which feeds perception and reaches no revenue or margin driver by design.
  • The shape checker cannot judge quality. It verifies that the workbooks are exactly what was asked for. Whether 47,320 is a good number for Home Depot is a question no check in this repository can answer.

Final command: py -m forecaster.final_run

Expected output: four completed workbooks in submission/, one timestamped log in logs/, one results file per company under runs/, and exit code 0.
